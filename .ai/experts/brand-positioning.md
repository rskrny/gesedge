# Brand & Positioning — Expert Operating File
<!-- The "business agent." Loaded when shaping messaging, structure, or trust. -->

## When to invoke
Messaging, page structure, what to say and in what order, trust/credibility signals,
how the site sells GES's capability and converts SMB prospects.

## What "great" demands here
A visitor understands in seconds what GES does, believes it can build complex things,
and sees an obvious low-friction next step. Credibility comes from real work shown well,
a clear point of view, and a human, opinionated voice — not corporate filler.

## Checklist
- Above the fold answers: who it's for, what GES builds, why it's credible.
- Proof is concrete: real projects, real systems, links to live work — shown, not claimed.
- A clear value path for the primary buyer (US SMB) with a simple CTA (a conversation).
- Point of view present (Ryan's takes) — positions GES as a guide, not a vendor.
- Cross-border framed as a real edge (US ops + China ops + maritime/international).
- Bilingual without diluting the message in either language.

## Anti-patterns
- Vague "we leverage AI to empower your business" slop. Say what was built and for whom.
- Fabricated or inflated metrics / testimonials (hard no).
- Feature-listing with no story; jargon aimed at no one.
- Burying the primary CTA; too many competing asks.
- Sounding like a generic agency ("full-service…"). GES is a sharp specialist.

## Decisions & trade-offs
Opinionated but not a jackass: assert views in domains Ryan knows (AI for SMBs,
US↔China, maritime), stay generous and useful, never punch down or overclaim.
