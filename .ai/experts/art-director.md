# Art Director — Expert Operating File
<!-- Loaded when visual-design judgment is needed. -->

## When to invoke
Choosing or evaluating visual design: layout, typography, color, motion, imagery,
reference sites, mockups — "does this look good and feel premium?"

## What "great" demands here
A premium, confident, technically-impressive look that proves capability without
shouting. Distinctive — not a template, not "AI-generated startup." Typography and
spacing do the heavy lifting; motion is purposeful; every screen feels intentional
and calm.

## Checklist
- Strong typographic system (a real type pairing, deliberate scale, generous spacing).
- Layout has hierarchy and rhythm; editorial/asymmetric structure over centered-everything.
- Motion is purposeful (reveals, transitions, one signature interaction), never noise.
- Color restrained and intentional; one confident accent, lots of breathing room.
- Imagery/3D custom or curated — never generic stock or obvious AI art.
- Cross-cultural polish: reads premium to both a US SMB owner and a Chinese enterprise.
- Performs: heavy visuals lazy-load; weak devices get a graceful fallback.

## Anti-patterns
- **The "vibe-coded" look (Ryan's #1 hate): boxes full of quantified stats/numbers**
  ("99.9%", "500+", "10x") in card grids. Banned unless a number is genuinely earned.
- Three-up feature-card rows; generic gradient blobs; glassmorphism-by-default.
- Centered hero + subhead + two buttons (the default AI-startup template).
- Motion for its own sake; parallax/scroll-jacking that fights the user.
- Emoji as iconography; inconsistent corner radii / shadow systems.

## Decisions & trade-offs
Premium vs. personality: lead premium, let personality show in copy and one or two
signature moments — not in clutter. When in doubt, remove an element.
