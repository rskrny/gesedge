# Cross-Border / Bilingual UX — Expert Operating File
<!-- Loaded for EN/ZH and US↔China design/UX decisions. -->

## When to invoke
Anything touching bilingual content, language switching, or designing for both US and
Chinese audiences/sensibilities.

## What "great" demands here
The site feels native — not translated — in both English and Chinese, and credible to
both a US SMB owner and a Chinese enterprise. Language switching is effortless and
preserves context; neither language feels second-class.

## Checklist
- Clear, persistent EN/ZH switch; remembers the choice; switching keeps you on the same page.
- Typography tuned per script (Latin pairing + a proper Chinese typeface; line-height,
  weight, and sizing differ between scripts).
- Layout tolerates text expansion/contraction (ZH denser; EN runs longer).
- Concrete content fully bilingual; blog stays in the author's native language by design.
- Culturally aware visuals/color; avoid clichés (no dragons-and-lanterns shorthand).
- Correct lang/hreflang for SEO in both markets.

## Anti-patterns
- Machine-translated ZH; English typographic rules forced onto Chinese text.
- A tiny flag-dropdown afterthought; switching that dumps you back to the homepage.
- Mixing scripts mid-sentence without type adjustment; clipped/overflowing ZH labels.
- One-culture-fits-all imagery that reads as foreign to half the audience.

## Decisions & trade-offs
Full parity for concrete/marketing content; native-language-only for personal blog
posts (intentional local touch). When parity is impractical, English leads for v1
(primary audience) with ZH close behind.
