## How to use this
Don't copy any mark here — extract the PRINCIPLE. The cure for "looks AI-generated" is a single
**proprietary structural move**, which every lane below converges on. Pick a direction, design 2-3
originals, then run each through the render→vision pipeline (`.ai/experts/ui-design-review.md`).

## Four convergent principles (what all 7 lanes agree on)
1. **One gesture, not a scene or a grid.** Reduce to a single joint / stroke / cut. It's the only thing
   that survives at 16px (favicon) AND animates cleanly. The clip-art globe dies the moment you stop
   drawing the whole ball (Telemundo & Pan Am were literally rescued this way).
2. **Carry the West↔East duality in a structural device with a warm→cool SEAM.** The best "connection"
   marks don't draw a bridge — they draw the *joint* where two forms meet, and let that seam carry the
   meaning and the warm(West)→cool(East) color shift. The meeting point IS the logo.
3. **Mark and wordmark share DNA.** Derive the symbol from a structural feature of the "GES" letterforms
   (a cut, an edge, a joint), so the mark reads *engineered*, not generated. One proprietary move beats
   decoration. For bilingual: pair by a shared geometry both scripts inhabit (a frame/coin/threshold),
   not by font-matching — then under-weight the denser CJK.
4. **Animate by construction that settles into the exact frozen mark.** One continuous object across
   states (loader draws → hero owns → scroll docks into nav via FLIP — "state, not swap"). Give it
   *material* with an emissive surface + bloom in a lit scene, never a CSS glow.

## Three directions for an ORIGINAL mark (synthesized — for Ryan to pick)
- **A — "The Seam" (evolve the Beam).** Keep globe + warm→cool great-circle span, but make the *joint*
  the hero (principle 2): two nodes (warm West / cool East) and the focal seam where they blend; globe
  reduced to ONE meridian gesture, not a grid. Off-the-shelf structural match: globe.gl `emit-arcs`.
  Lowest risk — but on its own still risks "globe + arc = default." Needs a proprietary twist to escape.
- **B — "The 寰桥 Gate / Threshold."** Bilingual-native: a symmetrical gate/doorway or horizon glyph
  (Shang Xia's threshold, NIO's horizon) reading as a portal between East/West, warm→cool split L/R.
  A coin/seal frame (Bank of China) makes a dense Hanzi like 寰 read at favicon size. Animates open on
  load. **Most distinctive / strongest antidote to "AI-generated."**
- **C — "The Edge-Cut Monogram."** Derive the symbol from a structural cut in the "GES" wordmark — the
  edge/joint where two letters meet becomes the bridge motif (Global *Edge*). Mark = wordmark DNA
  (Perplexity/Linear playbook). Lightest to build, most peer-credible, most scalable. Risk: least overtly
  "world/bridge" — pair it with the 成都寰桥 lockup to carry the cross-border meaning.

**Recommendation:** develop **B + C** as the two fresh contenders (they best answer "make it not look
AI-generated"), keep **A** as the safe hybrid/fallback. Render all chosen marks through the vision pipeline.

### 7. 3D / shader / emissive material marks (WebGL / R3F)
*Principle: a mark reads as a physical object — not flat gloss — when light INTERACTS with it: an emissive surface pushed above the bloom luminance threshold in a lit scene, real depth, warm→cool baked into emission (not a CSS glow).*
- **globe.gl `emit-arcs`** — https://github.com/vasturiano/globe.gl/blob/master/example/emit-arcs-on-click/ — a globe emits animated great-circle arcs — *Tool:* three-globe (TubeGeometry arcs) — **closest structural match to our mark; very light** — 5/5
- **R3F bloom-HDR / glass-flower** — https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/getting-started/examples — emissive mesh blooming in a lit scene — *Tool:* R3F + drei + react-postprocessing Bloom — the exact recipe for a warm→cool emissive bridge; poster fallback trivial — 5/5
- **Codrops "Dreamy Particles" (GPGPU)** — https://tympanus.net/Tutorials/DreamyParticles — particles ASSEMBLE onto a 3D model, glow by velocity + bloom — *Tool:* three.js GPGPU/FBO — particle-assembly into the mark; gate to capable GPUs, poster on mobile — 5/5
- **Paper Shaders — Mesh Gradient** — https://shaders.paper.design/mesh-gradient — animated warm→cool mesh-gradient backdrop — *Tool:* `@paper-design/shaders-react` (already our stack) — the field the mark sits inside; near-zero cost, CSS-static fallback — 5/5
- **Codrops "High-Speed Light Trails"** — https://tympanus.net/codrops/2019/11/13/high-speed-light-trails-in-three-js/ — glowing shader-lit tube trails — *Tool:* three.js TubeGeometry + ShaderMaterial — the glowing-tube technique for the span; ~1 draw call — 4/5
- **drei MeshRefractionMaterial** — http://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/shaders/mesh-refraction-material — refractive / glass / chrome 3D — *Tool:* R3F/drei + CubeCamera — a glass-bridge variant; moderate GPU — 4/5
- **Stripe gradient (minigl, reverse-engineered)** — https://kevinhufnagl.com/how-to-stripe-website-gradient-effect/ — FBM mesh-gradient, viewport-gated — *Tool:* minigl/GLSL — proven premium backdrop with a built-in viewport gate — 4/5
- **Top 3 for GES:** globe.gl `emit-arcs` (literal glowing great-circle + globe) · R3F bloom-HDR (warm→cool emissive done right) · Codrops Dreamy Particles (mark assembly).
